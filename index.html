02 / Time

A coastline with layers.

The supplied source traces Dahab from ancient coastal use through Nabataean, Roman, Byzantine, Islamic, Ottoman and modern periods.

Ancient → Nabataean

Dahab’s coast was used as an anchorage and settlement area, with archaeological remains dating back to ancient times. From the 1st century BCE to 106 CE, the region was influenced by the Nabataean Kingdom, an important trading power.

Roman → Byzantine

From 106 to 330 CE, Roman rule followed Rome’s annexation of the Nabataean Kingdom. During Byzantine rule (330–641 CE), Dahab featured a fortified coastal complex and harbor.

British occupation / protectorate

The source places Dahab within the period of British occupation/protectorate of Egypt.

A turning point

1982 → 1990s

Sinai returned to Egypt in 1982, a major turning point in Dahab’s modern development. The supplied source says Dahab developed rapidly as an international diving and tourism destination during the 1990s.

The source also notes that tourism was temporarily disrupted by terrorist attacks in Sinai between 2004 and 2006, including the 2006 Dahab bombings.

04 / Culture

Desert gold, made by people.

Food, craft and music form a living cultural layer connected to Bedouin traditions of Sinai.

Food

Regional dishes

Arisha cheese, Bedouin bread, rice and meat dishes, dates, herbal teas and Arabic coffee. Food is closely connected with hospitality, family gatherings and celebrations.

Craft

Traditional crafts

Bedouin embroidery, weaving, beadwork, leatherwork and handmade jewelry use colorful patterns and geometric designs.

Sound

Traditional music

Bedouin poetry, singing, drums and the rababa can accompany weddings, celebrations, gatherings and storytelling.
